# Proposed Operating Model

**Status:** pre-pilot design. This document describes the intended service shape; it does not authorise operations. Details may change as professional review and pilot design progress.

## 1. Request

A person would use a private channel to request a defined form of support.

Possible request types, subject to the approved pilot scope, may include:

- accompaniment on an ordinary necessary journey or to an appointment;
- support in obtaining essential medicines or everyday supplies through a lawful, pre-agreed route;
- a bounded social visit or check-in where isolation itself is the need;
- another ordinary practical support request that remains within the programme's approved civilian role.

Only the minimum information needed to decide whether the service can safely help should be collected. Public posts or comments must never be used for exact addresses, recurring routes, medication details or sensitive personal circumstances.

## 2. Triage

Every request would be classified before volunteer assignment.

### Accept in principle

A planned, non-emergency request appears capable of being supported safely within the programme's approved role, safeguarding arrangements and current pilot scope.

### Refer elsewhere

The primary need is better served by police, emergency services, healthcare, pharmacy, social care, domestic-abuse services, professional transport, licensed security or another specialist provider.

### Decline

The request would require a role, authority or professional judgement outside the programme boundary.

### Emergency

The person is directed to the appropriate emergency route. Volunteers are not dispatched as a substitute emergency response.

A materially changed request must be assessed as changed. An earlier acceptance is not inherited automatically merely because the same person, route or support type is involved.

## 3. Medicine and healthcare boundary

Safe Passage may eventually support someone **around** healthcare without becoming a healthcare service.

Examples may include accompanying a beneficiary to a medical appointment or, where specifically approved, helping them obtain prescribed medicine through a lawful collection route.

Any medicine-related activity must be defined before pilot use and must satisfy the exact legal, insurance, safeguarding, consent, identity and pharmacy/provider requirements that apply to it. Volunteers must not diagnose, recommend treatment, change dosages, administer medication, manage a person's medication regime or make clinical decisions.

If the actual need requires healthcare or professional-care judgement, the request is referred or handed over rather than stretched to fit Safe Passage.

## 4. Volunteer matching

The proposed default is two eligible volunteers for in-person support.

Before sensitive assignment details are released, the future system should confirm:

- that the request has passed triage;
- that the volunteers are currently eligible and available;
- that relevant conflicts of interest have been checked;
- that each volunteer receives only the information required for the assignment;
- that responsibility for communications is clear;
- that the assignment has reached a confirmed state before the beneficiary is told it is covered.

Loss of a volunteer does not silently turn a two-person assignment into an authorised one-person assignment. Any future exception requires a separately approved governance and safeguarding rule.

## 5. Pre-support confirmation

Before an assignment begins, the approved process should confirm the current consent and contact route, assigned volunteers, agreed location or meeting point, expected completion window, accessibility or safeguarding instructions, check-in method, cancellation route and police/emergency/professional-service thresholds.

For any approved medicine or essential-item collection, the process must also confirm the specific authorisation and provider requirements before a volunteer is given the task.

A failed mandatory condition produces an explicit hold, re-match, referral, cancellation or escalation decision rather than an assumed pass.

## 6. During support

Volunteers would:

- remain focused on the person requesting support;
- behave calmly, lawfully and without intimidation;
- preserve the person's dignity and ordinary decision-making agency;
- provide only the form of support that was accepted;
- avoid unnecessary engagement with third parties;
- move away from avoidable danger;
- contact police, emergency, healthcare, safeguarding or specialist services when the programme boundary is crossed;
- follow lawful instructions from emergency or police services where relevant.

For social-company or check-in requests, the same safeguarding, privacy, conduct and closure rules apply. Company does not create entitlement to enter a home, remain after consent is withdrawn, assume a care role, or form an unmanaged dependency.

Any programme identification used in a future pilot must be professional, politically neutral and incapable of reasonable confusion with police, military or security uniform.

## 7. Change and escalation thresholds

If circumstances change during an assignment, volunteers follow the approved plan and use the appropriate handover route.

A future operating framework should distinguish at least:

- **continue normally** — no material change to the accepted conditions;
- **continue with caution** — a minor change exists but the support remains safely within scope;
- **change plan / withdraw / hold** — the assignment is no longer safely manageable under the accepted plan;
- **contact police, healthcare, safeguarding or specialist service** — the event requires assessment, authority or capability outside the programme;
- **call 999** — there is immediate danger to life, serious violence, a medical emergency or another urgent emergency.

Volunteers must not remain in danger merely to complete an assignment.

## 8. Completion

Every accepted support request requires an explicit end state, whether that is ordinary completion, agreed handover, referral, cancellation or emergency transfer.

The future process should record only what is necessary to establish that responsibility is closed and to preserve any complaint, safeguarding or other duty that remains open.

An assignment is not complete merely because communication stops.

## 9. Feedback and review

Routine feedback should concentrate on service quality rather than praise:

- Did the agreed support happen as planned?
- Was the person treated with dignity and choice?
- Were service boundaries maintained?
- Did anything occur that requires follow-up?
- Should a similar future request be accepted, modified or referred elsewhere?

Beneficiaries must not be pressured to provide testimonials or publicity.

## 10. Repeated support

Recurring support creates additional privacy, predictability, boundary and dependency risks. It should therefore be reviewed rather than renewed indefinitely by default.

Review should consider whether another service is more appropriate, whether routes or schedules are becoming predictable, whether volunteer-beneficiary boundaries remain sound, whether the person is gaining confidence or becoming dependent on a particular volunteer, and whether a wider safeguarding, health, care or community issue has emerged.

Repeated social-company requests require particular care around consent, dependency, lone access, gifts, money, personal relationships and the boundary between ordinary company and a role better owned by a professional or established community service.

## 11. Pilot boundary

This model remains provisional until professional safeguarding, insurance, legal, data-protection and relevant police/community-safety, healthcare/pharmacy or specialist advice has been incorporated into a defined pilot plan for the activities actually proposed.
