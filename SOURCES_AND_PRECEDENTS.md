# Sources and Precedents

**Live-checked:** August 2026  
**Purpose:** keep a small set of public examples and official sources that have informed parts of the Safe Passage design.

Existing schemes are useful comparators, not endorsements of Custodian Safe Passage. Each example below is included for the practical lesson it may offer, with the differences kept clear.

## S-001 — Liverpool Village Angels pilot

**Primary source:** Merseyside Police, “Merseyside Police and Police Commissioner supports new Liverpool ‘Village Angels’ scheme,” published February 2026.  
**Source:** https://www.merseyside.police.uk/news/merseyside/news/2026/february-2026/merseyside-police-and-police-commissioner-supports-new-liverpool-village-angels-scheme/

### What the source shows

Merseyside Police describes an initial eight-week Liverpool trial of the LGBT Foundation’s volunteer-led Village Angels programme. Volunteers operate during defined night-time hours and locations, support vulnerable people, provide practical help and signposting, and are connected with police and city-centre partners through defined communication arrangements.

### Potentially transferable design features

- a time-limited pilot before assumptions about continuation;
- a defined operating area and hours;
- visible but clearly non-police volunteer identification;
- practical support and reassurance rather than enforcement;
- established communication routes with police and other partners;
- review before longer-term expansion.

### Limits

Village Angels is a night-time patrol/support model in a specific city-centre environment. Custodian Safe Passage proposes pre-arranged support around individual needs. The delivery body, funding, beneficiaries, operating environment and risk profile differ.

Support for Village Angels does not imply support, approval or transferability for Custodian Safe Passage. Safe Passage requires its own professional review and operating framework.

## S-002 — Merseyside Police Support Volunteer assurance process

**Primary source:** Merseyside Police, “Police support volunteers.”  
**Source:** https://www.merseyside.police.uk/police-forces/merseyside-police/areas/careers/join-us/Police_Support_Volunteers/

### What the source shows

Merseyside Police’s volunteer process includes an initial application, interview, vetting, health assessment with consideration of reasonable adjustments, and formal registration before the volunteer role begins.

### Potentially transferable design features

- application and interview before access to a role;
- suitability assessment rather than relying on self-description or prior credentials;
- vetting as one part of assurance rather than the whole process;
- reasonable-adjustment consideration;
- formal role entry only after the required process is complete.

### Limits

Police Support Volunteers operate inside a police force under police governance. Similar assurance steps would not make a Safe Passage volunteer a police volunteer, police employee, accredited person or holder of police powers.

## S-003 — Community Safety Accreditation Scheme governance logic

**Primary source:** Merseyside Police, “Our services — Development of Community Safety Accreditation Schemes.”  
**Source:** https://www.merseyside.police.uk/police-forces/merseyside-police/areas/au/about-us/our-services/

### What the source shows

Merseyside Police describes Community Safety Accreditation Schemes as operating through carefully monitored, jointly agreed protocols covering operational arrangements, information sharing, joint working, performance monitoring and working relationships.

The source also makes clear that CSAS is a statutory accreditation framework under the Police Reform Act 2002 and that accredited persons may, where appropriate, receive limited powers.

### Potentially transferable design features

The useful comparator for Safe Passage is the governance discipline, including:

- written operational boundaries;
- agreed interfaces between organisations;
- controlled information sharing;
- review and accountability;
- explicit responsibility and working relationships.

### Limits

Custodian Safe Passage is not presented as a CSAS scheme and its volunteers are not assumed to possess accredited or police powers.

## S-004 — Strategic and operational role separation

**Primary source:** Merseyside Police and Crime Commissioner, information about the Commissioner and the Police and Crime Plan.  
**Sources:**

- https://www.merseysidepcc.info/about-your-commissioner/about-your-pcc/
- https://www.merseysidepcc.info/down-to-business/how-we-work/police-and-crime-plan/

### What the sources show

The Police and Crime Commissioner publishes a Police and Crime Plan setting strategic policing and community-safety priorities. This is a useful public example of separating strategic community-safety direction and accountability from operational policing and other delivery roles.

### Potentially transferable design feature

Safe Passage should give each external organisation only the questions and decisions that belong to its actual role rather than treating public-service organisations as interchangeable.

### Limits

This is an institutional-routing and governance comparator only. It does not imply that a Police and Crime Commissioner, police force or other public body has assessed or endorsed Custodian Safe Passage.

## S-005 — Merseyside Police and Crime Plan 2025–2029

**Primary source:** Merseyside Police and Crime Commissioner, “Police and Crime Plan.”  
**Source:** https://www.merseysidepcc.info/down-to-business/how-we-work/police-and-crime-plan/

### What the source shows

The current plan identifies priorities around crime and anti-social behaviour, supporting victims and preventing harm.

### Relevance

A bounded community-support proposal may reasonably be discussed alongside public priorities concerning prevention, vulnerable people, victim support and community safety where those areas genuinely overlap with the proposed service.

### Limits

Similarity to a published priority does not imply approval, funding eligibility, operational suitability, police support or effectiveness.

## Source-use rules

Before a source from this register is used in a proposal, public claim or professional discussion:

1. reopen the live primary source;
2. confirm that the organisation, timing and relevant statement still match the intended use;
3. distinguish what the source says from what Safe Passage proposes to learn from it;
4. state important differences between the comparator and Safe Passage;
5. do not use a public body’s support for another programme as implied support for this one;
6. prefer primary or official material where available;
7. update or retire an entry when its source or underlying scheme materially changes.

> Use precedents to learn, not to borrow authority.
