# Custodian Safe Passage

**A proposed community-support and safe-accompaniment initiative for ordinary needs that can become difficult to manage alone.**

**Current status:** public-development copy · pre-pilot · external consultation and framework refinement · not recruiting

> **Pre-pilot note:** the service model is still being developed. Details may change as safeguarding, legal, insurance, data-protection and delivery work progresses.

Custodian Safe Passage is being developed as a small, bounded community-support programme. The core idea is simple: when someone is vulnerable, isolated or would benefit from a reassuring second person, a suitably verified and trained volunteer team may be able to provide calm, practical human support within a clearly defined role.

That future support may include, where the relevant safeguarding, legal, insurance and operational rules permit it:

- accompanying someone on an ordinary necessary journey or to an appointment;
- helping someone obtain essential medicines or everyday supplies through a lawful, pre-agreed collection or accompaniment route;
- providing ordinary social company or a planned check-in where isolation itself is the need;
- helping the person reach the service that properly owns a need outside Safe Passage's role.

The programme is intended to sit **before the point at which police, emergency, statutory-care, healthcare or other specialist professional powers are required**. It supports existing services and hands over cleanly when another service owns the need.

## Development stage

By **mid-August 2026**, the project had moved beyond an internal concept into a structured public framework and external feedback stage.

Exploratory discussions and meetings have taken place with relevant policing and community-safety contacts, and practical feedback has also been received through tri-service and veteran contacts. Those conversations have already informed the design, particularly around the civilian role, safeguarding, volunteer boundaries, accountability, escalation and handover.

Follow-up engagement is continuing or planned while that feedback is converted into a bounded, professionally reviewed pilot framework. These contacts are development inputs and do not by themselves imply formal partnership, endorsement, approval or institutional adoption.

## Proposed service shape

A future pilot would use a controlled process:

1. a person privately makes a service request;
2. the request is checked against the programme's scope and safeguarding boundaries;
3. approved volunteers are matched only after the request is accepted;
4. the volunteers provide only the agreed form of help, remain focused on the beneficiary and preserve calm, dignity and ordinary agency;
5. the service request is explicitly closed, referred or escalated through the appropriate route, with concerns recorded where required.

Two-volunteer attendance is the proposed default for in-person support. Any exception would require its own approved safeguarding and governance rule before operational use; a missing preferred condition does not silently lower the standard.

## Role boundary

The programme's role is **beneficiary-requested civilian support and accompaniment**. Volunteers remain ordinary members of the public operating within defined training, conduct, safeguarding and escalation rules.

Safe Passage is not a clinical or professional-care service. Volunteers do not diagnose, advise on treatment, administer or manage medication, or replace healthcare, social care, professional transport, pharmacy, police, emergency, licensed-security or other specialist responsibilities. Any future medicine-related support must follow the relevant consent, identity, provider/pharmacy, legal, insurance and safeguarding requirements for the exact activity.

Where an accepted request crosses into a professional or statutory domain, the programme's job is to refer, withdraw or hand over appropriately rather than improvise a new role.

## Who it is for

Subject to professional safeguarding advice and the capacity of any future pilot, the service is intended to be open on the basis of **need and safe deliverability**.

Requests would be considered against the support needed, safeguarding requirements and what the programme can safely provide.

## Safeguarding comes before scale

No pilot should begin until the project has suitable arrangements for:

- named safeguarding and operational responsibility;
- volunteer application, identity checks, references, role-appropriate vetting and training;
- complaints, restriction and removal procedures;
- insurance and legal review appropriate to the actual activities offered;
- data protection, retention and secure communications;
- healthcare, pharmacy, police, safeguarding and specialist-service referral boundaries where relevant;
- incident and near-miss reporting;
- a defined pilot area, service scope, capacity, review point and stop conditions.

Previous military, emergency-service or professional experience may be relevant background. Present-day suitability, vetting, training, conduct and supervision remain the operating standard.

## Public development record

The public SITREP is intentionally event-driven rather than performative. It records material progress, external engagement, narrowing, setbacks and stop decisions while keeping personal data, beneficiary information, private correspondence, operational routes and internal red-team material out of the public repository.

Public updates use role-level or institutional descriptions where that is enough to explain progress. Broad timing may be used to show development sequence, while personal names, exact meeting dates and locations stay out unless genuinely necessary and proportionate.

## Project support and external relationships

Safe Passage has already received practical development input through relevant policing/community-safety and tri-service/veteran contacts. Further professional interest, advice, practical backing or collaboration may also develop as the framework matures.

Any formal partnership, endorsement, approval or organisational role will be described only when it has actually been established and the public wording can be supported directly.

Custodian Safe Passage is a distinct community-benefit initiative supported by **[Custodian Systems](https://mark45cdo-mkuft.github.io/Custodian-Systems/)**, which can provide technical, administrative and systems-design support.

Safe Passage retains its own mission, safeguarding boundaries, service criteria, complaints routes and stop conditions. The support relationship is designed to strengthen delivery without transferring those responsibilities away from the programme.

## Emergency and police contact

In the UK, call **999** where there is immediate danger to life, serious violence, a medical emergency or another emergency requiring an urgent response. Non-emergency police matters should use **101** or the relevant police force's official reporting route.
