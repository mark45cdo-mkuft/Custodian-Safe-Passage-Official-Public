# Governance and Safeguarding Framework

**Status:** working pre-pilot design for professional review. It is not legal advice and does not authorise recruitment or operations. Details may change as the service model is refined.

## 1. Named responsibility

Before any pilot, responsibility should be assigned in writing for at least:

- project leadership;
- safeguarding;
- volunteer coordination;
- data protection and information handling;
- complaints, restriction and suspension decisions;
- police/community-safety liaison;
- healthcare, pharmacy or other specialist liaison where the approved activity set requires it;
- deputy arrangements when a named lead is unavailable.

Serious incidents must not depend on an unowned shared inbox or informal assumption that somebody else is dealing with them.

## 2. Volunteer assurance

The final standard must be set with appropriate professional advice. The working model includes:

- confirmed identity and eligibility to volunteer;
- structured application and interview;
- independent and verifiable references;
- criminal-record or police vetting at the level lawfully available and appropriate to the actual role;
- conflict-of-interest declarations where relevant to safe service;
- driving, insurance and vehicle checks if transport is ever separately introduced;
- reasonable-adjustment discussion relevant to safe performance;
- signed conduct and confidentiality requirements;
- probation or supervised initial activity;
- periodic revalidation.

Military, emergency-service, security or other professional experience may be useful background. It does not bypass present-day checks or create authority within the programme.

## 3. Training baseline

Before assignment, volunteers should understand:

- the service mission and its boundaries;
- absence of police or enforcement powers;
- safeguarding adults and children;
- consent, dignity and communication;
- conflict avoidance and safe withdrawal;
- emergency, police, healthcare and specialist-service thresholds;
- confidentiality and data minimisation;
- equality and non-discrimination;
- professional and personal boundaries;
- home/social-visit boundaries where those activities are in scope;
- medicine and pharmacy boundaries where those activities are in scope;
- gifts, money, dependency and private-contact risks;
- incident, near-miss and complaint reporting;
- how to refuse or stop an unsafe assignment.

## 4. Pairing and vulnerable persons

- Two-volunteer attendance is the proposed default for in-person support.
- A single volunteer must not become the automatic fallback when the second volunteer is unavailable.
- No volunteer should be placed alone with a child through this programme.
- Work involving children or adults at risk requires an approved safeguarding pathway.
- Parent, guardian, carer or responsible-agency involvement must be defined where required.
- The programme must not create informal unsupervised care relationships.
- Repeated matching with the same beneficiary requires boundary review.
- A social visit or check-in does not by itself authorise entry to a home; consent, location, access and lone-working rules must be defined before any such activity is offered.

Any future exception to the proposed paired-attendance rule requires an explicit, professionally reviewed framework before use.

## 5. Conduct standard

Volunteers must:

- act calmly, lawfully and without intimidation;
- respect the beneficiary's choices unless an immediate safeguarding or emergency duty overrides ordinary choice;
- provide only the support that was accepted and approved;
- avoid political, religious, romantic, financial or commercial solicitation;
- avoid alcohol or impairing substances before and during duty;
- disclose relevant conflicts and previous relationships;
- use only approved communication routes for operational information;
- preserve confidentiality;
- avoid borrowing, lending, accepting significant gifts or handling beneficiary money outside any specifically approved procedure;
- avoid forming unmanaged private arrangements or dependencies with beneficiaries;
- report concerns through the approved route.

## 6. Medicine and healthcare boundary

Safe Passage may support someone around healthcare only through activities explicitly included in the approved pilot scope.

Any future medicine-related activity requires the relevant legal, insurance, safeguarding, consent, identity and pharmacy/provider conditions to be defined before use. Volunteers do not diagnose, recommend treatment, change dosage, administer medication, manage medication regimes or make clinical decisions.

Prescription or medicine information should be treated as sensitive. Volunteers should receive only what is necessary for the approved task and should not retain copies, labels, photographs or details beyond the authorised process.

If the actual need requires professional healthcare or care judgement, Safe Passage refers or hands over rather than stretching the volunteer role.

## 7. Social company and home-visit boundary

Ordinary company or a planned check-in may be part of the intended service where isolation itself is the need, but it requires the same safeguarding discipline as any other in-person activity.

Before such support is offered, the programme must define:

- location and access rules;
- pairing or any professionally approved exception;
- consent and withdrawal;
- check-in/check-out and missed-contact procedures;
- gifts, money and personal-contact boundaries;
- repeat-visit and dependency review;
- when a need should move to social care, healthcare, befriending, advocacy or another established service.

Company is human support, not an informal care contract or private friendship assigned by the programme.

## 8. Fair and consistent service

The programme is intended to serve people fairly and consistently within its approved scope.

Requests and volunteer suitability are considered against the support needed, safeguarding requirements, role requirements and what the programme can safely provide. The same service boundaries apply consistently.

## 9. Prohibited conduct

Immediate restriction or suspension must be available for matters such as:

- violence, threats or confrontation-seeking;
- carrying a weapon for the assignment;
- impersonating police, military authority or security personnel;
- discriminatory conduct;
- stalking, harassment or inappropriate personal contact;
- unauthorised photography, recording, surveillance or intelligence gathering;
- publishing beneficiary, medication, home-access or route information;
- bypassing the programme to create private recurring arrangements;
- unauthorised handling of medication, money or property;
- dishonesty in vetting, records or incident accounts;
- retaliation against a complainant or witness;
- using the programme to recruit into another organisation, ideology, campaign or commercial service.

## 10. Complaints and safeguarding reports

A future system must provide:

- a route independent of the volunteer complained about;
- immediate safeguarding triage;
- acknowledgement and a recoverable case reference;
- preservation of relevant records;
- temporary restriction or suspension where proportionate;
- police, healthcare, pharmacy, safeguarding or statutory referral where required;
- a recorded outcome and review route where lawful;
- protection against retaliation.

A complaint is operational and safeguarding information and should be handled through the approved route.

## 11. Incident and near-miss review

Reviews should ask whether an event exposed:

- immediate harm;
- boundary failure;
- training failure;
- unsuitable matching;
- data exposure;
- volunteer conduct problems;
- environmental, home-access or route risk;
- medicine, money, gifts or dependency risk;
- unclear police/healthcare/pharmacy/specialist liaison;
- a reason to pause or change the service.

Repeated small failures can reveal a structural problem even when no single event causes serious harm.

## 12. Independent review

Before scaling, the programme should seek external safeguarding or community-safety review from a person or body able to assess the model independently of project leadership.

## 13. Relationship with Custodian Systems

Custodian Systems may provide technical, administrative and systems-design support. That support must not override safeguarding decisions, complaints, eligibility criteria, incident classification or stop conditions.

Support infrastructure can help the programme work; it must not become an alternative authority over the people the programme exists to serve.

## 14. Expansion gate

Expansion requires review showing that the pilot remained within scope, volunteer selection and removal worked, complaints could be made safely, personal information remained controlled, partner responsibilities were clear, activity-specific safeguards worked, and any benefit was not purchased by transferring hidden risk elsewhere.
