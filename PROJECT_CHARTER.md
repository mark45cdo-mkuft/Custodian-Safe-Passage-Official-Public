# Project Charter

## Mission

Custodian Safe Passage exists to help people meet ordinary, necessary day-to-day needs with greater safety, confidence, dignity and human support by connecting them, where appropriate, with suitably verified and trained volunteers operating within strict boundaries.

## Public value

The project addresses a narrow gap between:

- a person being too vulnerable, isolated or practically unsupported to manage an ordinary need comfortably alone; and
- the point at which police, emergency, statutory-care, healthcare or another specialist professional capability is required.

The aim is to add a trustworthy human presence while keeping each service at its proper role and handing over cleanly when another service owns the need.

Possible future support, subject to approved safeguarding and delivery rules, may include:

- accompaniment on an ordinary necessary journey or to an appointment;
- support in obtaining essential medicines or everyday supplies through a lawful, pre-agreed route;
- ordinary social company or a planned check-in where isolation itself is the need;
- practical support in reaching the appropriate statutory, healthcare, safeguarding or community service where Safe Passage is not the correct owner.

## Intended beneficiaries

Subject to professional safeguarding advice and pilot capacity, a future service may support people such as:

- older people;
- vulnerable adults;
- people who feel unsafe or unable to manage a recurring or necessary journey alone;
- people who need support reaching an appointment or obtaining an essential item;
- people experiencing isolation who would benefit from a bounded, planned social visit or check-in;
- parents or carers;
- young people only where an approved safeguarding route and appropriate responsible-adult arrangements exist;
- other residents whose request falls within the defined service criteria.

Requests would be considered against the support needed, safeguarding requirements and what the programme can safely provide.

## Governing object

The beneficiary and the safe completion, referral or lawful closure of the accepted support request remain the purpose of the service.

Project leadership, volunteers, partners, publicity and organisational growth are all subordinate to that purpose.

## Initial delivery model

A future pilot is intended to be:

- small and geographically bounded;
- time-limited and reviewed before expansion;
- based on private pre-arranged support requests;
- delivered by two volunteers as the proposed default for in-person support;
- supported by clear intake, matching, consent, safeguarding, communication and completion controls;
- connected to healthcare, pharmacy, police, emergency, safeguarding and specialist services at defined boundaries where relevant;
- reviewed for both benefit and transferred risk.

Any departure from the proposed two-volunteer standard requires an explicitly approved exception framework before use. Absence of the preferred condition is not automatic permission to operate at a lower standard.

## Service boundary

Custodian Safe Passage is a **civilian community-support and accompaniment service**. Its operating role is to support an accepted beneficiary request through calm presence, practical assistance within the approved scope, appropriate communication, safe withdrawal where needed, and clean referral or handover when another service owns the problem.

Safe Passage is not a clinical or professional-care service. Volunteers do not diagnose, advise on treatment, administer or manage medication, or replace healthcare, social care, pharmacy, professional transport, licensed security, police, emergency services or other specialist organisations.

Any future medicine-related support must be limited to an activity that has been specifically approved and is lawful, insured, safeguarded and compatible with the relevant consent, identity and provider or pharmacy requirements. No medicine-related wording in this charter authorises current collection, transport, handling or administration.

## Governing principles

### Person first

The person requesting support remains the purpose of the activity. Their dignity, agency and privacy take priority over publicity, recruitment or organisational reputation.

### Voluntary participation

Participation by beneficiary and volunteer is voluntary. Consent may be withdrawn, subject only to any immediate safeguarding or emergency duties that arise.

### Minimum necessary role

Volunteers remain ordinary members of the public operating within the programme's approved role. Training and prior experience support good judgement; they do not create a separate authority.

### Fair and consistent decisions

Requests are considered against the support needed, safeguarding requirements and what the programme can safely provide. The same service rules and boundaries apply consistently.

### Boundaries before expansion

The programme must demonstrate that it can preserve safeguarding, privacy, conduct and accountability at pilot scale before increasing geography, membership or service scope.

### Accurate public claims

Partnership, approval, effectiveness, readiness and safety outcomes are described only to the level actually established.

### Learn and improve

Complaints, near misses and failures must be recorded and allowed to change the model. Unsafe volunteers, activities, practices or assumptions are suspended, narrowed or removed when required.

## Relationship with Custodian Systems

Custodian Safe Passage is a distinct community-benefit initiative. **[Custodian Systems](https://mark45cdo-mkuft.github.io/Custodian-Systems/)** may support it with technical work, administration, systems design and practical problem-solving.

Safe Passage retains its own mission, safeguarding duties, eligibility criteria, complaints routes and stop conditions. Commercial or organisational interests remain subordinate to those duties.

## Pilot success conditions

A pilot should be considered promising only where review shows:

- safe completion or appropriate referral of accepted support requests;
- beneficiary dignity, choice and confidence;
- reliable volunteer attendance and conduct;
- effective maintenance of the civilian support role;
- proportionate liaison with healthcare, pharmacy, police, safeguarding and other specialist services where relevant;
- secure handling of personal, location and support information;
- usable complaints, restriction and removal procedures;
- a clear indication that the service adds value beyond existing provision;
- no concealed transfer of unreasonable risk to beneficiaries, volunteers, partners or the wider public.

## Stop conditions

Operational activity must pause where a material condition such as the following is unresolved:

- safeguarding arrangements are inadequate;
- required insurance or legal status for the actual activity is not established;
- sensitive information cannot be protected;
- volunteer control, supervision or removal is unreliable;
- a proposed support activity exceeds the approved volunteer role or requires professional judgement or authority;
- participation or demand begins moving the service outside its defined role or safe capacity;
- a healthcare, pharmacy, police, safeguarding or professional partner identifies a serious unresolved risk relevant to the activity;
- demand exceeds the programme's safe delivery capacity.

A stop condition is part of responsible operation and responsible development.
