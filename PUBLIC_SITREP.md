# Public Development SITREP

**Purpose:** a sober public record of material development progress for Custodian Safe Passage.  
**Current stage:** pre-pilot · external consultation and feasibility review · not recruiting

> **Pre-pilot note:** the service model is still being developed. Details may change as safeguarding, legal, insurance, data-protection and delivery work progresses.

This log exists so that the project can show how it is developing without publishing private correspondence, personal data, beneficiary information, operational routes, security-sensitive detail or internal red-team material.

Updates are made when the project changes materially. Routine internal work does not need a public entry merely to create activity.

## Current public development baseline — early September 2026

### Completed / established so far

- established a clean public-facing documentation set separate from the private development and provenance record;
- published the current project charter, proposed operating model, governance and safeguarding framework, information-security boundary, and sources/precedents register;
- clarified the intended service as broader beneficiary-requested civilian support: potential future activities include accompaniment on ordinary journeys or appointments, support in obtaining essential medicines or everyday supplies through specifically approved lawful routes, and bounded social company/check-ins where isolation itself is the need;
- kept healthcare, medication, professional-care and statutory responsibilities with their proper owners: Safe Passage does not diagnose, advise on treatment, administer or manage medication, or inherit professional authority;
- completed initial exploratory contact and discussion with relevant policing and community-safety contacts to test the proposal against practical operational, safeguarding and accountability questions;
- received practical feedback through tri-service and veteran contacts, adding service, responsibility, community-support and public-facing experience to the development process;
- used those discussions to test an original design question: whether Safe Passage could make or support a bounded connection without then having to own or manage every ordinary human relationship that might naturally develop from it;
- identified that this organisational boundary may be harder to sustain in the present safeguarding, liability and accountability environment than the simple human idea itself;
- recorded a change in the policing/community-safety consultation route: a planned follow-up meeting on 25 August did not go ahead, and Police Scotland confirmed that the project details had been forwarded to **Safer Communities** for the next stage of discussion;
- defined request handling around need, safeguarding requirements and safe deliverability;
- defined Custodian Systems as a supporting technical and administrative partner while keeping Safe Passage's safeguarding mission and operating decisions distinct;
- retained the project at pre-pilot status while the professional, operational and feasibility questions remain unresolved;
- added a simple public-facing website prototype designed around clarity, accessibility, calm presentation and transparent development status.

### External engagement and feedback

By late August and early September 2026, exploratory discussions and meetings had taken place with relevant policing, community-safety, tri-service and veteran contacts as part of testing the proposal against real operational, safeguarding and accountability questions.

A planned follow-up meeting on **25 August 2026** did not go ahead. Police Scotland subsequently confirmed that the project details had been forwarded to **Safer Communities**, as discussed. The next policing/community-safety consultation route is therefore through Safer Communities rather than the cancelled meeting.

This is recorded as a development-route update only. It does **not** imply formal partnership, endorsement, approval, adoption or a commitment by Police Scotland or Safer Communities.

Further discussion through an experienced tri-service/veteran contact has also tested a question that was already part of the project from the beginning: whether a deliberately small and bounded service can responsibly make an initial connection and then allow ordinary human relationships to remain ordinary, rather than becoming indefinitely owned or supervised by the organisation that made the introduction.

The discussion was useful because it did not challenge the value of people helping one another. It highlighted how quickly a simple act of support can naturally become friendship, informal help or an ongoing relationship, and how difficult it may be for an organisation to define where its responsibility ends once that happens.

That creates a genuine feasibility question for Safe Passage. A model that carries too little responsibility may not provide an adequate safeguarding or accountability framework. A model that carries too much may become intrusive, bureaucratic or fundamentally different from the simple human support the project was intended to enable.

The project therefore needs to test not only whether appropriate policies can be written, but whether the organisational boundary itself can hold in practice without creating unacceptable transferred risk or damaging the informal human relationships that already work well in communities.

This is not a newly discovered issue. It is an original design question receiving a more serious answer from external discussion and practical experience.

These contacts are development inputs. They do not by themselves imply formal partnership, endorsement, approval or institutional adoption.

### In progress before any pilot

- professional safeguarding review and named safeguarding responsibility;
- appropriate insurance and legal review for the activities actually proposed;
- data-protection, retention and secure-communications arrangements;
- volunteer eligibility, identity assurance, role-appropriate vetting, training and removal procedures;
- complaints, incident and near-miss reporting routes;
- police, safeguarding, healthcare/pharmacy and specialist-service referral or activity boundaries where relevant;
- continued external discussion and review, including follow-up through Safer Communities;
- testing whether a bounded organisational role can remain genuinely bounded once ordinary human relationships develop beyond an initial accepted request;
- definition of a small pilot area, initial service scope, capacity, review point and stop conditions only if that feasibility question can be resolved responsibly.

### Current stage

Pilot service delivery has not begun. The descriptions above state the intended purpose, candidate service types and current development progress, not current operational readiness.

The project is **past the internal-concept-only stage**. It now has a structured public framework, an initial governance and safeguarding model, and real external discussion and feedback from relevant policing/community-safety and tri-service/veteran contacts.

That external discussion has now moved the project into a more fundamental feasibility review. The immediate question is no longer simply how to refine the existing model into a pilot-ready framework. It is whether the proposed organisational boundary can be made responsible and durable without either leaving unacceptable safeguarding and liability gaps or turning ordinary human support into something permanently supervised and institutional.

The underlying need and the value of people helping one another have not been rejected. The live question is whether Safe Passage, as an organisation, can support that human good without becoming responsible for a relationship it cannot realistically or appropriately own.

For now, the project will continue to listen, test and narrow rather than force a pilot timetable. If a responsible form exists that preserves the human value of the idea, the project will continue to test for it. If the controls required would fundamentally change the thing being built, that result will be recorded honestly rather than forcing the project forward.

## Public-log rules

For each future update:

1. state what materially changed;
2. distinguish completed work from proposed or unresolved work;
3. record meaningful setbacks, narrowing or stop decisions as well as progress;
4. carry material external engagement into the stage summary and completed/progress record rather than isolating it in a lower section;
5. describe external engagement at role, institutional, community or responsibility level when that is enough to establish the stage reached;
6. use broad development timing where useful, but keep personal names, exact meeting dates and locations out of the public SITREP unless genuinely necessary and proportionate;
7. do not publish personal data, beneficiary information, live support or route details, private correspondence, contact ladders or security-sensitive procedures;
8. describe partnership, endorsement, approval, authority and readiness only when they are directly established;
9. keep internal red-team analysis in the private development record and publish only the resulting public-safe rule or correction where it genuinely belongs.

> Transparency should show real development, external contact, what that contact changed and what comes next — without publishing the contact map.
