# Information Security and Data-Minimisation Framework

**Status:** pre-pilot design for professional data-protection review. Details may change as the operating model is refined.

## Core rule

Collect, disclose and retain only information that is necessary for a defined and lawful purpose at the current stage of the service.

A fact being useful does not automatically mean the programme is entitled to collect or retain it.

## Public information

A public repository or website may contain:

- mission and service boundaries;
- general eligibility information;
- emergency and alternative-service signposting;
- approved governance and safeguarding policies;
- public sources and precedents;
- anonymised aggregate evaluation where lawful and safe.

It must not contain:

- live or recurring support routes or exact visit locations;
- beneficiary names or identifiable stories without specific consent and a legitimate reason;
- volunteer home details or identity documents;
- exact shift or availability patterns;
- live assignment status;
- prescription, medication, health or pharmacy details linked to an identifiable person;
- home-access information, key locations or access codes;
- allegations about identifiable people;
- operational incident details that create avoidable identification or safety risk;
- passwords, keys, access tokens or internal security details.

## Information classes

A future operational system should distinguish at least:

### Public

Mission, boundaries, approved policies, public sources and general guidance.

### Internal administrative

Volunteer application status, training records, policy acknowledgements and ordinary contact administration.

### Restricted operational

Exact meeting or visit point, destination where relevant, time window, assigned volunteers, beneficiary contact details and the minimum practical information needed for the accepted support request.

### Safeguarding / health / incident restricted

Allegations, vulnerability or medical information, prescription or medicine information where legitimately required, child or adult-protection concerns, police references, complaint records and suspension records.

Access should narrow as sensitivity increases.

## Need-to-know release

Exact support information should not be released merely because somebody is interested in helping. A future system should release operational details only after the request has passed triage, the assignment is authorised, relevant conflicts have been checked and the recipient is entitled to receive that information.

Volunteers should receive the minimum information needed to deliver the specific assignment safely.

For an approved medicine-related task, that may include only the information required by the lawful collection or provider process. The programme should not collect a wider medical history merely because the assignment involves a pharmacy or prescription.

For an approved social visit or check-in, volunteers should not receive unrelated medical, family, financial or safeguarding history unless it is genuinely required for safe delivery and lawfully shareable.

## Communication channels

Sensitive operational information should not be handled through public posts, open volunteer chats, personal social-media messages, uncontrolled screenshots or channels where former members retain access.

Approved systems should support access removal, role-based access, incident preservation and recoverable audit where proportionate.

Medication labels, prescriptions, pharmacy communications, home-access instructions or other sensitive material must not be photographed, copied or retained on personal devices unless an approved process specifically requires and protects that handling.

## Retention

Before launch, the programme must define for each information category:

- why it is collected;
- the lawful basis for processing;
- who controls and processes it;
- who may access it;
- the retention period;
- deletion or anonymisation procedure;
- subject-access and correction route;
- breach response;
- police, healthcare, pharmacy or safeguarding disclosure conditions where relevant;
- backup and recovery arrangements.

Support histories, route histories, visit histories and medicine-related records must not be retained indefinitely merely because storage is cheap.

## Volunteer confidentiality

Volunteers must not use service information to:

- discuss beneficiaries socially;
- retain route, visit, prescription or medication information beyond the approved period;
- contact a beneficiary privately outside the authorised process;
- retain home-access details outside the approved process;
- support another organisation, cause or commercial service;
- search beneficiaries or third parties on social media;
- publish supposedly anonymous stories that remain realistically identifiable.

## Incident preservation

Where a complaint, safeguarding concern, suspected offence, medication or property discrepancy, or data breach arises, relevant information may need to be preserved rather than routinely deleted. Access should be restricted and disclosure should occur only through the appropriate safeguarding, legal, police, healthcare, pharmacy or data-protection route.

Preservation for an incident is not permission for wider circulation.

## Public-repository boundary

This repository is intended to contain only public-facing policy, design status and public-source references. It is not an operational database.

No live beneficiary, volunteer-assurance, support assignment, location, prescription/medication, complaint, safeguarding or private correspondence record belongs here.

## Relationship with Custodian Systems

Technical support from Custodian Systems must follow the same need-to-know and data-minimisation boundary. Providing infrastructure does not create entitlement to use Safe Passage information for unrelated commercial, research, marketing or analytical purposes.

Any future data-processing relationship must be documented according to the actual organisational and legal structure in use at that time.
